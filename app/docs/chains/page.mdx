# Chains & Ecosystems

Notes on the broader blockchain landscape beyond just Ethereum and Solana development. Architecture, frameworks, and how different chains are positioned.

---

## Public vs Private Blockchain

| Type | Who Controls It | Examples | Use Case |
|------|----------------|----------|----------|
| Public L1 | Nobody (decentralized) | Ethereum, Solana, Bitcoin | Open DeFi, NFTs, permissionless apps |
| Public L2 | Company / DAO | Arbitrum, Base, Robinhood Chain | Cheaper Ethereum transactions |
| Public L3 | Anyone (via Orbit/OP Stack) | Custom app-chains | App-specific scaling |
| Private / Permissioned | Enterprise consortium | Hyperledger Fabric, Quorum | Enterprise, compliance-heavy |

---

## L1 vs L2 vs L3

**L1** is the base chain. Ethereum is an L1. It handles final settlement but is slow and expensive at scale.

**L2** sits on top of L1. Transactions execute on L2, then compressed proofs or transaction batches are posted to L1. Inherits L1 security at a fraction of the cost.

**L3** sits on top of L2. An app-chain customized for a specific use case — a game, a DeFi protocol, an exchange. Arbitrum Orbit lets you deploy L3s.

```
Ethereum (L1)          ← settlement, final security
  Arbitrum One (L2)    ← general purpose rollup
    My App Chain (L3)  ← via Arbitrum Orbit
```

## Two Rollup Approaches

| Approach | How It Works | Used By |
|----------|-------------|---------|
| Optimistic Rollups | Assume transactions are valid, allow fraud proofs | Arbitrum, Optimism, Base, Robinhood Chain |
| ZK Rollups | Cryptographic validity proof for every batch | zkSync, Starknet, Polygon zkEVM |

Optimistic rollups are easier to build and EVM-compatible. ZK rollups have faster finality but are more complex.
